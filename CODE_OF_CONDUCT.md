# Code of Conduct

## Short version

Argue with the **idea**. Don't argue with the **person**.

That's the whole rule. Everything below is just colour on that.

---

## Slightly longer version

ZEON is a research project. We have opinions, we are wrong sometimes,
and we expect to argue. Sharp, evidence-driven, technically-rigorous
disagreement is exactly what we want — that's how research works.

What we don't want:

- Personal attacks. "Your idea makes no sense because X" is fine.
  "You make no sense" is not.
- Insults based on identity, background, employer, school, country,
  age, or anything else that isn't the merits of the code or the
  argument.
- Sustained derailing. If you've been told once that the discussion
  has moved on, don't bring the same gripe back five threads later.
- LGTM-style review-bombing of PRs you disagree with politically.
- Posting private DMs publicly without consent.
- Reading nasty intent into a non-native English speaker's terse
  reply. This is a global project; charitable interpretation costs
  nothing.

What we *do* want:

- Concrete criticism, ideally backed by numbers or a code reference.
- "I tried this and got these numbers" is the gold standard.
- Self-aware uncertainty. "I'm not sure but it looks like …" beats
  "obviously …".
- Helping new contributors find their footing without making them
  feel small.

---

## Scope

This applies in:
- The GitHub repo (issues, PRs, discussions, comments).
- Any official ZEON chat (Discord, etc. — links live in the README
  once they exist).
- Any space where you're representing the project.

It does **not** govern your own personal Twitter / blog / band-name.
We don't police what you say outside this project.

---

## How we handle problems

Most things you'll see in this project will not be code-of-conduct
issues. They'll be people disagreeing intensely about a technical
question, which is fine. If you actually think a code-of-conduct
breach has occurred:

1. **First, try direct.** A short, calm "hey, that came across as
   personal — can we reframe?" comment resolves 90% of cases. People
   slip up; we're all reading text on a screen.

2. **If that doesn't work, escalate privately.** Contact the
   maintainers (see [README.md](README.md) for the current list of
   active maintainers) via DM or email. We'll handle it without
   making it public unless you ask us to.

3. **Maintainers can**, depending on severity:
   - Ask the person to edit a comment, take a breath, and try again.
   - Lock a thread that's spiraling.
   - Temporarily ban an account from the repo / chat.
   - Permanently ban, for sustained or severe violations.

Maintainers are also bound by this CoC. If you have a problem with a
maintainer, contact a *different* maintainer.

---

## What we do **not** do

- We don't ban people for being terse, blunt, or non-native English.
- We don't ban people for being wrong (we'd run out of maintainers).
- We don't ban people for opinions about ML research that we
  disagree with.
- We don't publicly shame.
- We don't keep dossiers.

---

## Inspirations

This is a deliberately short CoC. The longer Contributor Covenant
v2.1 ([source](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/))
covers the same ground in more detail, and we are happy to consider
ourselves bound by its spirit even where it's more specific than what
we wrote down here.
